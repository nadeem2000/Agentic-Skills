# 25 Practical AI Workflows for Development Organisations

**A practical starting point for NGOs, INGOs, development consulting firms, evaluation organisations, research teams and development professionals**

Generative AI creates more organisational value when it is attached to a **defined workflow** than when staff are simply given a list of prompts.

The 25 examples below are not recommendations to automate everything. They are candidates for structured experimentation. Each organisation should assess value, feasibility, data sensitivity, consequence of error, evidence requirements and human oversight before implementation.

## A. Proposal, business development and resource mobilisation

### 1. Opportunity qualification

Convert a new call, RFP or TOR into a structured opportunity note covering client, geography, scope, eligibility, deadlines, estimated effort, strategic fit and immediate questions.

**Human review:** Verify every material requirement against the original solicitation.

### 2. RFP / TOR decomposition

Break a complex solicitation into work packages, deliverables, implied methods, staffing requirements and submission instructions.

**Human review:** Procurement and legal interpretations require source-level checking.

### 3. Proposal compliance matrix

Create and maintain a matrix connecting each requirement to the response section, evidence source, responsible contributor and final verification status.

**Human review:** A proposal manager owns the final compliance check.

### 4. Institutional experience retrieval

Search approved organisational records for projects relevant to a new opportunity and map verified experience against selection criteria.

**Human review:** Never infer a donor, budget, result, role or country experience that is not documented.

### 5. Donor opportunity fit assessment

Compare a funding opportunity with organisational mandate, geography, thematic expertise, delivery capacity and partnership requirements.

**Human review:** Verify current donor rules, eligibility and deadlines from official sources.

### 6. Expert / CV alignment

Compare a consultant's verified CV with TOR competency requirements and identify strong evidence, gaps and areas requiring clarification.

**Human review:** Do not inflate years, roles, qualifications or assignment experience.

## B. Research and evidence

### 7. Authoritative source strategy

Define which official, institutional and peer-reviewed sources should be prioritised for a research question before broad searching begins.

### 8. Literature-screening assistant

Screen titles, abstracts or documents against explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria and create a review queue.

**Human review:** Borderline studies and methodological quality remain researcher decisions.

### 9. Evidence extraction matrix

Extract claims, methods, dates, geographies, populations, definitions and source locations into a structured comparison table.

### 10. Conflicting-statistics review

Compare multiple estimates by year, definition, population, geography, methodology and intended use rather than selecting the most convenient number.

### 11. Institutional and stakeholder mapping

Structure the mandates, authority, geographic level, implementation role and relevance of ministries, agencies, donors, implementers and civil-society actors.

**Human review:** Current institutional mandates must be verified where change is possible.

## C. Monitoring, evaluation and learning

### 12. Evaluation TOR analysis

Turn an evaluation TOR into a structured assignment interpretation covering purpose, scope, questions, deliverables, methods, data needs, stakeholders and risks.

### 13. Evaluation question-to-method matrix

Map evaluation questions to indicators, evidence sources, methods, respondent groups, triangulation needs and limitations.

**Human review:** Qualified evaluators own methodological decisions.

### 14. Research-tool drafting support

Draft interview guides, focus-group guides, observation tools or survey items from an approved evaluation framework.

**Human review:** Ethics, safeguarding, local language and respondent burden require expert review and piloting.

### 15. Qualitative synthesis support

Organise coded or de-identified qualitative material into themes, contradictions, outliers and evidence summaries.

**Human review:** Do not treat model summaries as primary evidence or disclose sensitive raw data to unapproved systems.

### 16. Finding-to-evidence traceability

Create a matrix connecting each draft finding to supporting and contradictory evidence, sources, confidence and unresolved gaps.

### 17. Evaluation report QA

Review a draft report for internal contradictions, unsupported claims, missing triangulation, inconsistent terminology and broken links between evidence and conclusions.

## D. Programme design and management

### 18. Programme logic stress test

Review a theory of change or results framework for missing assumptions, weak causal links, unrealistic dependencies and measurement gaps.

### 19. Risk-register development

Turn programme documents and contextual evidence into a structured list of implementation, safeguarding, fiduciary, political, climate, operational and data risks for human validation.

### 20. Implementation decision brief

Synthesize progress reports, monitoring information and unresolved issues into a concise management brief separating evidence, interpretation, decisions and follow-up actions.

## E. Organisational knowledge and learning

### 21. Institutional knowledge assistant

Retrieve answers from approved organisational policies, project documents, past proposals, evaluations and technical resources with direct links to the source material.

**Human review:** Access permissions, document currency and confidentiality are core controls.

### 22. Lessons-learned synthesis

Compare lessons across projects or evaluations and distinguish recurring patterns from one-off observations.

### 23. Meeting-to-action workflow

Convert approved meeting notes or transcripts into decisions, action owners, deadlines, dependencies and unresolved questions.

**Human review:** Participants should validate consequential decisions before they are treated as agreed.

## F. Communications and professional productivity

### 24. Evidence-grounded communications workflow

Convert an approved report or dataset into audience-specific briefs, web copy, social posts or presentation content while preserving the underlying evidence and avoiding exaggerated claims.

### 25. Development professional workbench

Create a reusable assistant for an evaluator, researcher, proposal specialist or programme manager that routes common tasks through appropriate evidence, quality and human-review steps.

# How to choose where to start

Do not begin with the most technically impressive use case. A useful pilot normally has:

- a recurring workflow;
- a clear pain point;
- accessible and sufficiently reliable inputs;
- low or moderate consequence of error;
- staff willing to test the process;
- measurable success criteria;
- a clear human owner.

A simple prioritisation method is to score each candidate on:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Value | Would improving this workflow materially help the team? |
| Frequency | Does the task recur often enough to justify standardisation? |
| Feasibility | Are the documents, data and process sufficiently accessible? |
| Risk | What happens if the AI is wrong? |
| Sensitivity | Does the workflow involve confidential or personal data? |
| Verifiability | Can important outputs be checked against authoritative sources? |
| Ownership | Is a human role accountable for the final output or decision? |

Prioritise high-value, feasible workflows with manageable risk before attempting high-consequence automation.

# Five responsible-AI rules for every workflow

1. **Treat AI output as unverified until checked.**
2. **Do not expose sensitive information to tools that are not approved for it.**
3. **Keep a human accountable for consequential outputs and decisions.**
4. **Require source traceability where factual or evidence claims matter.**
5. **Measure the pilot before claiming efficiency, quality or impact gains.**

# From a workflow list to an organisational system

The next step is not to copy all 25 workflows into a chatbot. It is to select a small number of high-value cases, map the current process, identify failure modes, build reusable instructions and quality gates, test with real users, and refine the workflow before wider adoption.

# Grant & Bid Opportunity Intelligence Specialist

## AI-supported opportunity discovery, qualification and capture for development organisations and consulting firms

## Purpose

This flagship solution demonstrates how development organisations, NGOs, INGOs, resource-mobilisation teams and consulting firms can use generative AI to **find, verify, understand, qualify and prioritise opportunities before committing significant proposal or bid-development resources**.

It deliberately separates two different opportunity pathways:

1. **Grant and institutional-funding opportunities** — typically pursued through Calls for Proposals, Calls for Concept Notes, grant RFPs, challenge funds, innovation funds, partnership calls and other funding windows.
2. **Consulting and professional-service opportunities** — typically pursued through bids, tenders, EOIs, prequalification notices, consulting RFPs/TORs, framework agreements and other service procurements.

The two pathways share an intelligence discipline, but they **do not use the same qualification logic**.

> **Core principle:** Opportunity intelligence can support faster and more disciplined screening, but eligibility, strategic fit, commercial commitment and pursue/no-pursue decisions remain human responsibilities.

## Portfolio role

This Specialist sits **upstream** of the [AI Proposal & Bid Development Workflow](../ai-proposal-bid-development/README.md).

```text
Find & qualify opportunities
        ↓
Human pursue / do-not-pursue decision
        ↓
Develop proposal / concept note / EOI / bid
        ↓
Human approval and submission
```

The opportunity-intelligence stage answers **whether an organisation should pursue an opportunity and what must be prepared next**. It does not replace the downstream work of developing the actual concept note, proposal, EOI or bid.

## Two professional analyst tracks

### Track A — Grant Opportunity Analyst

For:
- NGOs and INGOs;
- foundations and civil-society organisations;
- universities and research institutions;
- resource-mobilisation and institutional-funding teams;
- programme-development and partnerships teams.

Primary question:

> **Are we eligible, strategically aligned, credible to deliver, appropriately partnered and competitive for this funding opportunity?**

### Track B — Consulting Bid Analyst

For:
- international-development consulting firms;
- evaluation firms;
- research and advisory companies;
- technical-assistance providers;
- business-development, bid and capture teams.

Primary question:

> **Can we satisfy the procurement requirements, demonstrate relevant corporate experience, mobilise the required experts and partners, manage delivery risk, and compete credibly for the assignment?**

## Shared intelligence discipline

Both tracks use the same high-level sequence:

```text
Discover
   ↓
Verify
   ↓
Classify
   ↓
Qualify
   ↓
Match
   ↓
Prioritise
   ↓
Capture
   ↓
Human decision
```

The shared sequence creates consistency. The **criteria inside each stage remain track-specific**.

## Grant vs Consulting Bid — the distinction

| Dimension | Grant / institutional funding | Consulting bid / professional service |
|---|---|---|
| Typical opportunity formats | Call for Proposals, Call for Concept Notes, grant RFP, challenge fund, innovation fund, partnership/funding call | Tender, bid notice, EOI, prequalification, consulting RFP/TOR, framework agreement |
| Primary applicant | NGO, INGO, CSO, foundation, university, programme organisation or eligible consortium | Consulting firm, evaluation/research firm, advisory company or eligible consortium/JV |
| Core qualification question | Are we eligible, strategically aligned and credible to deliver the funded intervention? | Can we comply, demonstrate relevant corporate experience, mobilise experts and deliver the assignment competitively? |
| Key evidence | Organisational mandate, thematic/geographic experience, programme results, partnerships, legal eligibility | Corporate assignments, client references, service methodology, expert availability, firm credentials |
| Partnership logic | Often programme-delivery, localisation, geographic reach or thematic complementarity | Often competency gaps, local presence, JV rules, specialist expertise or mobilisation requirements |
| Financial considerations | Funding ceiling/range, co-financing, cost share, eligible costs, duration | Commercial attractiveness, level of effort, taxes, reimbursables, pricing conditions and delivery cost where management provides inputs |
| Downstream submission | Concept note, full proposal, application, partnership proposal | EOI, technical proposal, financial proposal, bid, prequalification submission |
| Final decision owner | Management / grants / programme leadership | Management / BD / capture / commercial leadership |

# Track A — Grant Opportunity Analyst

## Typical grant opportunity formats

The Analyst should preserve the terminology used by the source. A funding opportunity might be a:

- Call for Proposals;
- Call for Concept Notes;
- grant RFP;
- challenge fund;
- innovation fund;
- institutional funding window;
- thematic or country programme call;
- partnership funding call;
- restricted or invited funding opportunity.

Not every grant opportunity should be labelled an RFP.

## Stage A1 — Opportunity discovery

**AI-supported tasks**

- structure searches across approved donor and institutional sources;
- classify opportunities by donor, theme, geography, instrument and deadline;
- identify obvious duplicates;
- create a preliminary opportunity register;
- flag opportunities that fall outside agreed search parameters.

**Human responsibility**

- define donor, sector, geography and funding priorities;
- approve the sources being monitored;
- decide which opportunities merit deeper verification.

## Stage A2 — Official-source verification

**AI-supported tasks**

- identify the official donor or institutional source;
- capture the authoritative opportunity page or document reference;
- extract deadline, funding range, duration and key dates;
- identify guidance notes, templates and annexes;
- flag discrepancies between secondary alerts and official material.

**Human responsibility**

- confirm all material facts from official sources;
- resolve discrepancies before internal circulation or pursuit.

**Quality gate**

A newsletter, aggregator, social post or third-party alert may help discover an opportunity, but it does not become the authoritative source for eligibility, deadline, funding or submission requirements.

## Stage A3 — Eligibility assessment

**AI-supported tasks**

- extract applicant eligibility;
- identify country/geographic restrictions;
- identify legal-status and registration requirements;
- identify consortium or partnership conditions;
- identify co-financing/cost-share requirements where stated;
- identify mandatory thematic or prior-experience conditions;
- create an eligibility matrix with source references.

**Human responsibility**

- make the authoritative eligibility determination;
- seek donor clarification where eligibility remains ambiguous;
- prevent a positive AI summary from overriding explicit donor rules.

## Stage A4 — Strategic fit

**AI-supported tasks**

- compare donor priorities with organisational strategy;
- assess thematic, geographic and beneficiary alignment;
- compare the call with current programme models and institutional mandate;
- identify where pursuit would require significant mission or capability stretch;
- structure reasons to pursue and reasons not to pursue.

**Human responsibility**

- decide whether the opportunity is strategically desirable;
- avoid opportunistic funding pursuit that pulls the organisation outside its mandate.

## Stage A5 — Organisational capability and evidence match

**AI-supported tasks**

- map verified organisational experience against the call;
- identify relevant projects, results, geographies, donors and partnerships from approved records;
- identify evidence gaps;
- distinguish documented capability from aspirational capability;
- create a source-linked capability matrix.

**Human responsibility**

- approve every organisational claim;
- decide whether capability gaps can be addressed credibly;
- confirm which past experience may be cited or shared.

## Stage A6 — Partnership and consortium requirements

**AI-supported tasks**

- identify mandatory partnership requirements;
- identify localisation, geographic-reach or thematic gaps;
- map partner-search criteria;
- structure a preliminary consortium rationale;
- flag partner dependencies that affect the pursue decision.

**Human responsibility**

- select and contact partners;
- assess reputational, fiduciary and delivery risk;
- agree roles and relationships;
- confirm partner eligibility and willingness.

## Stage A7 — Funding and submission pathway

**AI-supported tasks**

- summarise funding ceiling/range and duration;
- identify cost-share or co-financing conditions where stated;
- identify concept-note versus full-proposal stages;
- identify templates, annexes and portal requirements;
- map internal milestones backwards from the deadline;
- identify unusual submission conditions requiring management attention.

**Human responsibility**

- validate financial implications;
- decide whether cost-share or implementation conditions are feasible;
- approve internal resource allocation and submission ownership.

## Stage A8 — Grant opportunity prioritisation

**AI-supported tasks**

- structure a transparent fit/feasibility scorecard;
- compare eligible opportunities using approved criteria;
- surface assumptions and uncertainties;
- prepare a Grant Opportunity Brief;
- recommend a next action such as pursue, monitor, clarify or decline.

**Human responsibility**

- make the final pursue / do-not-pursue / monitor decision;
- decide capture ownership and proposal-development resource allocation.

## Grant Opportunity Brief — reusable structure

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Opportunity | Official opportunity title |
| Donor / institution | Funding organisation |
| Official source | Authoritative source URL/document reference |
| Opportunity type | Call for Proposals, Concept Note call, challenge fund, grant RFP, etc. |
| Deadline / key dates | Submission and clarification dates |
| Geography | Eligible countries/locations |
| Thematic scope | Priority themes and intended outcomes |
| Funding | Amount/range/ceiling as officially stated |
| Duration | Eligible implementation period |
| Eligibility | Applicant and consortium conditions |
| Submission pathway | Concept note, full proposal, stages, portal/templates |
| Strategic fit | Why the opportunity does or does not align |
| Capability match | Verified relevant organisational experience |
| Evidence gaps | Missing proof or weak capability areas |
| Partnership needs | Mandatory or strategic partner requirements |
| Key risks | Eligibility, strategic, operational, financial or timing risks |
| Recommended next action | Pursue / clarify / monitor / decline |
| Human decision/status | Management-approved status and owner |

# Track B — Consulting Bid Analyst

## Typical consulting opportunity formats

The Consulting Bid Analyst focuses on professional-service procurement such as:

- bid or tender notices;
- consulting RFPs;
- TOR-based assignments;
- EOIs;
- prequalification notices;
- framework agreements;
- rosters/framework opportunities;
- donor or government consulting procurements.

It should distinguish these from grant opportunities and from goods/works procurements that fall outside the target service model.

## Stage B1 — Opportunity discovery

**AI-supported tasks**

- structure monitoring across approved procurement sources;
- classify assignments by buyer, sector, geography, service type and deadline;
- create a consulting-opportunity register;
- identify obvious duplicates;
- distinguish consulting assignments from grants and goods/works opportunities.

**Human responsibility**

- define priority buyers, markets, sectors and service lines;
- approve the procurement sources being monitored;
- decide which opportunities merit detailed qualification.

## Stage B2 — Procurement-source verification

**AI-supported tasks**

- identify the authoritative procurement notice, TOR/RFP and annexes;
- identify amendments or corrigenda;
- capture procurement method and current stage;
- extract deadlines and clarification dates;
- flag outdated or secondary-source notices.

**Human responsibility**

- verify current requirements against the original procurement source;
- confirm amendments, submission instructions and procurement status.

## Stage B3 — Eligibility and mandatory requirements

**AI-supported tasks**

- extract firm eligibility;
- identify legal and registration conditions;
- identify geographic or nationality restrictions;
- identify mandatory certifications, turnover or experience thresholds where stated;
- identify consortium/JV rules;
- identify mandatory forms and declarations;
- structure a compliance-oriented eligibility summary.

**Human responsibility**

- determine authoritative compliance;
- involve legal, procurement or commercial expertise where required;
- decide whether a mandatory gap makes the opportunity non-viable.

## Stage B4 — Scope and TOR decomposition

**AI-supported tasks**

- decompose the assignment into work packages;
- identify deliverables, locations, timing and implied level of effort;
- extract stated evaluation criteria;
- identify methodology implications;
- distinguish explicit scope from interpretation;
- structure clarification questions.

**Human responsibility**

- approve the interpretation of scope;
- determine delivery implications;
- decide whether ambiguity creates unacceptable bid or implementation risk.

## Stage B5 — Corporate experience match

**AI-supported tasks**

- map verified assignments to sector, service type, geography, client and methodology requirements;
- identify strongest relevant references;
- identify corporate capability gaps;
- create a source-linked experience matrix;
- flag claims that lack approved organisational evidence.

**Human responsibility**

- approve project claims and references;
- decide whether corporate experience is sufficiently relevant and competitive;
- confirm permissions where client/project information is restricted.

## Stage B6 — Expert and staffing implications

**AI-supported tasks**

- extract required roles and competency requirements;
- compare verified expert profiles against role requirements;
- identify recruitment, partner or associate gaps;
- flag unrealistic mobilisation assumptions;
- structure an early staffing-risk view.

**Human responsibility**

- confirm expert availability, consent and commercial expectations;
- approve staffing and mobilisation strategy;
- decide whether key-expert gaps are resolvable before proposal investment.

## Stage B7 — Partner, consortium and delivery-risk assessment

**AI-supported tasks**

- identify local-presence, JV or consortium needs;
- map missing technical or geographic competencies;
- structure delivery, schedule, operational and security risks;
- identify dependencies requiring capture action;
- distinguish manageable gaps from likely bid blockers.

**Human responsibility**

- select partners and experts;
- assess reputational and commercial risk;
- approve consortium structure;
- decide whether delivery conditions are acceptable.

## Stage B8 — Bid prioritisation and capture

**AI-supported tasks**

- prepare a transparent opportunity score using approved criteria;
- surface reasons to bid and reasons not to bid;
- produce a Consulting Bid Intelligence Brief;
- structure next actions for client understanding, partner outreach, expert mobilisation and proposal preparation.

**Human responsibility**

- make the final bid/no-bid decision;
- approve commercial resource commitment;
- determine capture strategy and proposal leadership.

## Consulting Bid Intelligence Brief — reusable structure

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Opportunity | Official assignment title |
| Buyer / client | Procuring organisation |
| Official source | Authoritative procurement source |
| Procurement type / stage | EOI, RFP, tender, prequalification, framework, etc. |
| Deadline / clarification dates | Current verified dates |
| Geography / location | Assignment location and coverage |
| Sector / service type | Technical area and consulting service |
| Scope | Major work packages and responsibilities |
| Deliverables | Key outputs and schedule |
| Eligibility | Firm/JV/registration/mandatory requirements |
| Evaluation criteria | Criteria and weights where stated |
| Corporate experience | Verified relevant assignments |
| Expert requirements | Key roles and competencies |
| Consortium / partner gaps | Missing capability or local presence |
| Delivery / commercial risks | Operational, staffing, timing or commercial concerns |
| Capability gaps | Material gaps affecting competitiveness or compliance |
| Recommended next action | Bid / clarify / monitor / decline |
| Human decision/status | Approved bid/no-bid status and owner |

# AI vs Human Responsibility Matrix

| Activity | AI can support | Human responsibility remains |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunity discovery | search structuring, classification, duplicate identification | source strategy and market priorities |
| Source verification | identify official documents, compare secondary alerts | authoritative verification and discrepancy resolution |
| Eligibility | extract and structure requirements | legal/authoritative eligibility decision |
| Strategic/corporate fit | compare approved strategy and experience | determine desirability and competitiveness |
| Organisational experience | retrieve and map verified records | approve claims and reference use |
| Experts and partners | identify stated requirements and gaps | availability, consent, selection and due diligence |
| Opportunity scoring | calculate transparent scorecards using approved criteria | choose criteria, interpret results, final decision |
| Capture actions | structure next steps and dependencies | outreach, commitments, commercial decisions |
| Submission | identify downstream requirements | approve and submit through authorised process |

## Official-source and evidence-verification rules

1. **Discovery source is not automatically the authoritative source.**
2. **Deadlines, eligibility, funding values and procurement conditions must be verified from official material before action.**
3. **Amendments and corrigenda take precedence over superseded notices.**
4. **Organisational experience must come from approved institutional records.**
5. **Expert availability must never be inferred from a CV or historical engagement.**
6. **Partner credentials must be verified rather than assumed from reputation or prior collaboration.**
7. **Unknown information should be labelled unknown or requiring clarification.**
8. **Source conflicts should be surfaced, not silently resolved by the model.**
9. **Opportunity facts should carry enough source metadata for a human reviewer to re-check them quickly.**
10. **Final pursuit decisions remain attributable to a named human decision owner.**

## Transparent prioritisation — not win prediction

Opportunity scores can help teams compare many opportunities, but a score should remain **decomposable and explainable**.

### Grant track — possible dimensions

- eligibility;
- strategic alignment;
- thematic/geographic fit;
- demonstrated organisational capability;
- partnership readiness;
- funding/implementation feasibility;
- submission feasibility;
- uncertainty/competition where credible evidence exists.

### Consulting-bid track — possible dimensions

- eligibility/compliance;
- corporate experience fit;
- technical scope fit;
- expert availability;
- local/partner readiness;
- delivery feasibility;
- commercial attractiveness where management supplies relevant inputs;
- strategic client/market value.

A score is a **decision-support artefact**, not an objective probability of winning. Weighting should be approved by management and visible to reviewers.

## Risk and Control Matrix

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Stale opportunity information | Re-check official source and amendment status before circulation/action |
| Incorrect deadline | Verify current deadline from authoritative notice/document |
| Secondary-source error | Treat aggregators/alerts as discovery aids only |
| Eligibility misinterpretation | Trace eligibility to source text and require human determination |
| Fabricated organisational experience | Retrieve only from approved institutional records |
| Invented expert availability | Confirm directly with the expert or authorised staffing owner |
| Grant/bid misclassification | Classify opportunity type before applying qualification logic |
| Missed consortium/co-financing condition | Include partnership, JV and cost-share checks in mandatory screening |
| Overconfident score | Show dimensions, weights, assumptions and uncertainty; never label as win probability |
| Biased prioritisation | Review criteria for mission, market and inclusion bias; permit human override with rationale |
| Confidential pipeline leakage | Apply access, retention and approved-tool rules to sensitive opportunity data |
| Opportunity chasing outside mandate | Include strategic-fit and mandate checks before proposal investment |
| Duplicate opportunities | Use source/title/buyer/deadline matching and retain canonical opportunity record |
| Missed amendment/corrigendum | Re-verify procurement/funding page before final pursue decision |
| AI overconfidence | Treat analysis as advisory and require named human decision owner |

## Potential organisational benefits

A well-designed opportunity-intelligence workflow may support:

- more consistent opportunity screening;
- earlier identification of ineligible or strategically weak opportunities;
- better separation of grant and consulting procurement pipelines;
- stronger use of verified organisational experience during qualification;
- earlier identification of partner, expert and capability gaps;
- clearer management visibility over why opportunities are being pursued;
- more structured handoff into proposal, concept-note, EOI or bid development;
- reduced repetitive manual summarisation across opportunity notices.

These are **potential benefits**. Actual efficiency, decision quality or pipeline outcomes should be measured during organisational pilots.

## Suggested pilot

Pilot the two tracks separately so their performance can be evaluated independently.

### Grant Opportunity Analyst pilot

Use a defined set of official donor/institutional sources and:

1. identify and classify opportunities during a fixed period;
2. verify material details from official sources;
3. produce **5–10 Grant Opportunity Briefs**;
4. compare Analyst recommendations with grant/resource-mobilisation team decisions;
5. record source corrections, eligibility corrections and human rewrites.

### Consulting Bid Analyst pilot

Use a defined set of procurement sources and:

1. identify and classify consulting opportunities;
2. verify notice/TOR/RFP status and amendments;
3. produce **5–10 Consulting Bid Intelligence Briefs**;
4. compare Analyst recommendations with BD/capture decisions;
5. record capability, expert and partner gaps found before proposal investment.

### Pilot metrics

- percentage of surfaced opportunities correctly classified;
- number of material source-verification corrections;
- number of eligibility interpretation corrections;
- number of duplicate opportunities detected;
- proportion of briefs requiring major human rewrite;
- number of capability, partner or expert gaps identified before pursuit;
- user-rated usefulness;
- Analyst recommendation agreement/disagreement with human review;
- sensitive-data or confidentiality incidents;
- reusable intelligence records created.

## From opportunity intelligence to submission development

When management decides to pursue an opportunity, the handoff should be explicit.

### Grant pathway

```text
Grant Opportunity Brief
        ↓
Human pursue decision
        ↓
Concept note / full proposal planning
        ↓
Proposal development + QA
        ↓
Human approval + submission
```

```text
Consulting Bid Intelligence Brief
        ↓
Human bid/no-bid decision
        ↓
Capture + proposal architecture
        ↓
Technical / financial proposal development + QA
        ↓
Human approval + submission
```

## Engagement model

This Specialist can be adapted to an organisation's actual grants or business-development process. A practical engagement may include:

1. source and pipeline mapping;
2. opportunity taxonomy and screening rules;
3. grant and/or consulting-bid qualification criteria;
4. approved organisational evidence mapping;
5. reusable opportunity brief templates;
6. human decision gates and governance controls;
7. pilot configuration and testing;
8. staff training;
9. measurement and refinement.

The purpose is not to replace grants, resource-mobilisation, BD or capture professionals. It is to give them a more structured, evidence-aware and reusable opportunity-intelligence workflow.

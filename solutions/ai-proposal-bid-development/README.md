# AI Proposal & Bid Development Workflow

## Purpose

This flagship solution demonstrates how a development consulting firm, NGO, INGO or proposal team can use generative AI across a bid-development process **without delegating bid strategy, organisational claims, technical judgment, costing or submission authority to the model**.

The objective is to turn a fragmented and highly manual process into a more structured workflow with reusable instructions, evidence checks and human approval points.

## Target users

- business-development and capture teams;
- proposal managers;
- technical leads;
- resource-mobilisation teams;
- partnership managers;
- consulting-firm directors;
- independent proposal specialists.

## The organisational problem

A typical bid may require staff to interpret a long RFP or TOR, identify mandatory requirements, assess organisational fit, map partners and experts, retrieve relevant institutional experience, structure a technical response, align CVs, coordinate inputs and conduct final compliance checks under a short deadline.

AI can support many of these tasks, but only if the workflow protects against fabricated requirements, invented experience, unsupported technical claims, stale donor information and loss of accountability.

## Workflow

### Stage 1 — Opportunity qualification

**AI-supported tasks**

- summarise the opportunity;
- extract geography, sector, assignment type, client and submission requirements;
- identify obvious eligibility conditions;
- structure a preliminary fit assessment.

**Human decision**

Bid/no-bid remains a management decision based on strategic fit, relationships, capacity, competition, cost and delivery risk.

### Stage 2 — RFP / TOR analysis

**AI-supported tasks**

- decompose scope of work;
- identify deliverables and implied work packages;
- identify evaluation criteria where stated;
- separate mandatory requirements from desirable characteristics;
- flag ambiguous or missing information.

**Quality gate**

Every material requirement is checked against the original solicitation. AI summaries never replace the source document.

### Stage 3 — Compliance matrix

Create a structured matrix covering:

- instruction or requirement;
- source section/page;
- response location;
- responsible person;
- evidence required;
- status;
- final verification.

The matrix becomes the control layer for the submission.

### Stage 4 — Capture and solution strategy

**AI-supported tasks**

- structure client/problem analysis;
- organise win themes supplied by the team;
- compare known strengths against evaluation criteria;
- identify gaps requiring partners, experts or additional evidence;
- prepare questions for the capture discussion.

**Human decision**

The model does not invent competitive intelligence or determine the winning strategy.

### Stage 5 — Institutional experience and evidence retrieval

**AI-supported tasks**

- search an approved internal corpus;
- retrieve relevant project experience;
- map prior assignments to current requirements;
- identify evidence gaps;
- prepare a source-linked experience matrix.

**Control**

Only verified organisational experience may appear in the proposal. No project, donor, value, country, role or result is inferred when it is not documented.

### Stage 6 — Proposal architecture

AI can help construct a response structure that maps:

RFP requirements → evaluation criteria → technical sections → evidence → responsible contributor.

Typical sections may include understanding, methodology, work plan, management, team, quality assurance, risk, safeguarding/inclusion and organisational experience, depending on the solicitation.

### Stage 7 — Draft development support

AI may help:

- turn approved notes into coherent draft sections;
- maintain terminology consistency;
- compare narrative against the compliance matrix;
- identify unsupported claims;
- shorten or restructure text to meet limits;
- flag duplication and contradictions.

The technical lead owns the substantive approach and final language.

### Stage 8 — Expert and CV alignment

AI can support a structured comparison of:

- TOR competency requirements;
- proposed role;
- verified qualifications;
- sector experience;
- country experience;
- assignment-specific evidence.

It should not inflate years of experience or manufacture responsibilities.

### Stage 9 — Final quality assurance

Final checks may cover:

- compliance completeness;
- cross-section consistency;
- names, dates and numbers;
- donor/client terminology;
- evidence support;
- staffing consistency;
- deliverable consistency;
- missing attachments;
- unresolved assumptions.

### Stage 10 — Human approval and submission

A responsible person confirms:

- all material requirements were verified against the solicitation;
- organisational claims are accurate;
- technical and financial proposals are aligned;
- permissions and representations are valid;
- required approvals have been obtained;
- the submission package is complete.

AI does not submit autonomously unless an organisation has explicitly designed and authorised such a process and the platform permits it.

## What this solution can improve

Potential benefits include:

- faster first-pass interpretation of solicitations;
- more systematic compliance management;
- easier retrieval of relevant institutional experience;
- better reuse of approved proposal knowledge;
- more consistent drafting and terminology;
- earlier identification of gaps and contradictions;
- reduced repetitive coordination work.

These are **potential benefits**. Actual time, quality or win-rate improvements should be measured during pilots rather than assumed.

## Key risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Fabricated RFP requirement | Trace every requirement to the source solicitation |
| Invented organisational experience | Retrieve only from approved institutional records |
| Stale donor information | Verify current requirements from official sources |
| Hallucinated citations | Require source-linked evidence and manual checking |
| Confidential bid data leakage | Apply organisational data-handling rules and approved tools |
| Over-reliance on generic methodology | Technical lead owns methodology and contextual adaptation |
| Inconsistent staffing information | Cross-check proposal, CVs, organogram and work plan |
| False confidence in AI output | Treat model output as draft/advisory until reviewed |

## Suggested pilot

A consulting firm can pilot this workflow using one **completed or low-risk live opportunity**.

Measure:

- time spent by workflow stage;
- number of compliance gaps found before submission;
- number of factual/consistency corrections required;
- user-rated usefulness;
- proportion of AI-generated content requiring major rewrite;
- sensitive-data or evidence incidents;
- reusable assets created for future bids.

# AI Integration Case Studies

These case studies are designed to show how reusable AI workflows can support real development-sector work.

The purpose is **not** to present speculative automation as proven impact. Each case separates:

- what was actually built or observed;
- what the AI-enabled intervention is designed to do;
- what organisational benefits are plausible but not yet measured;
- what controls are required before operational use.

## Case-study structure

Each case should use the following format:

1. **Challenge** — the recurring organisational problem.
2. **Existing process** — how the work is normally handled.
3. **AI-enabled intervention** — where AI is introduced.
4. **Workflow** — the sequence of steps, roles and hand-offs.
5. **Controls** — evidence, data, privacy, safeguarding and human-review requirements.
6. **Observed result** — only results directly supported by evidence.
7. **Potential organisational benefit** — clearly labelled where not yet measured.
8. **Transferability** — where the approach may apply elsewhere.
9. **Related tools / skills** — links to the technical evidence base.
10. **Contact** — route for organisations interested in adapting the workflow.

## Responsible claims policy

A case study must not:

- invent a client, assignment, metric or outcome;
- imply endorsement by an organisation that has not provided it;
- present estimated time savings as measured results;
- disclose confidential or proprietary information;
- imply that AI replaced professional accountability where it did not.

Where evidence is incomplete, use **potential benefit**, **illustrative workflow**, or **demonstration** rather than a claim of proven impact.

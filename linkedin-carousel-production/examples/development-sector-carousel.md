# Example: Development-Sector Evidence Carousel

## Scenario
A consulting firm wants to turn a long evaluation report into a concise LinkedIn carousel for senior development-sector decision-makers.

## Objective
Show how evaluation findings can be converted into practical management decisions without oversimplifying the evidence.

## Audience
NGO/INGO leaders, programme directors, evaluators, donor-facing teams, and development consultants.

## Recommended document title
**What Evaluations Tell Us About Programme Sustainability**

## Carousel

### Slide 1 — Hook
**Why good projects still fail to last**

Five sustainability questions every programme team should answer before the final evaluation.

**Visual direction:** Strong typographic cover. Use one simple visual metaphor—such as a bridge, chain, or lifecycle—rather than a generic stock image. Keep organization identity small.

### Slide 2 — Reframe the issue
**Sustainability is not a final-report question**

It is shaped much earlier by design choices, ownership, financing, capacity, and the systems expected to continue the intervention.

**Visual direction:** Five connected nodes representing those factors.

### Slide 3 — Question 1
**Who owns the result after the project ends?**

A handover plan is not enough. Look for clear institutional responsibility, decision rights, and incentives to continue the work.

**Visual direction:** Responsibility map with “project team” transitioning to “local institution / partner / system.”

### Slide 4 — Question 2
**What keeps it financed?**

Separate activities that need continuing external finance from those that can be absorbed into routine budgets, community mechanisms, fees, or partner systems.

**Visual direction:** Simple funding-flow diagram. Avoid decorative money icons.

### Slide 5 — Question 3
**Can the system operate without project specialists?**

Training numbers alone do not prove capacity. Examine role clarity, staff turnover, supervision, tools, institutional memory, and whether the required skills are embedded in normal operations.

**Visual direction:** “Training delivered” on the left versus “capacity institutionalized” on the right.

### Slide 6 — Question 4
**Is there evidence that people will keep using it?**

Adoption during implementation may reflect incentives or intensive support. Test whether the service or practice remains useful, accessible, affordable, and trusted after that support reduces.

**Visual direction:** User journey or adoption curve with a visible post-project transition point.

### Slide 7 — Question 5
**What happens when conditions change?**

Sustainable interventions can adapt. Look for local problem-solving capacity, feedback loops, data use, and the ability to adjust delivery without waiting for the original project team.

**Visual direction:** Feedback loop diagram.

### Slide 8 — Synthesis
**A stronger sustainability test**

Before asking, “Will the project continue?” ask:

- Who owns it?
- Who pays for it?
- Who can operate it?
- Who will keep using it?
- How will it adapt?

**Visual direction:** Five-question framework arranged in a clean circle or vertical stack.

### Slide 9 — CTA
**Use these five questions before the evaluation—not after it.**

They are more useful when they influence programme design, partnership agreements, budgets, monitoring, and transition planning.

**CTA:** Save this framework for your next programme review.

**Visual direction:** Minimal closing slide with the five-question framework reduced to a compact motif.

## Design system
- Canvas: 1080 × 1350 px, 4:5 portrait.
- Tone: evidence-led, professional, restrained.
- Typography: one strong sans-serif family plus optional compatible secondary face.
- Layout: consistent headline zone, flexible content zone, small source/footer zone.
- Visuals: diagrams and simple explanatory graphics rather than generic photography.
- Branding: apply supplied logo/colors if available; do not invent them.

## LinkedIn caption
Many programme teams start discussing sustainability when the final evaluation is already underway.

By then, several of the decisions that determine continuity have already been made: who owns the intervention, how it is financed, whether capacity is embedded, whether users will stay engaged, and whether the system can adapt.

This carousel turns those issues into five questions that can be used during programme design, implementation reviews, transition planning, and evaluation.

**Which of these five questions is most often missed in your experience?**

## Evidence note
This example intentionally demonstrates carousel structure rather than making project-specific empirical claims. When adapting a real evaluation report, replace generic statements with findings that are traceable to the supplied report and cite the relevant source where necessary.

## QA check
- Slide 1 contains a concrete problem and promise.
- Each body slide answers one distinct question.
- No unsupported statistics are introduced.
- The design uses explanatory visuals rather than filler.
- The CTA is singular and relevant to the audience.
- The caption complements rather than repeats the slides.

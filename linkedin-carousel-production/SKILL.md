# LinkedIn Carousel Production

## Purpose
Turn source material into a swipe-oriented LinkedIn document carousel that is concise, credible, visually coherent, and ready for publication. Produce the actual design when suitable design tools are available; otherwise deliver a complete content-and-design specification.

**REQUIRED REFERENCE:** Read `references/carousel-playbook.md` before producing the final carousel.

## 1. Determine the production mode
Use **Full production** when a connected design tool such as Canva can reliably create the multi-page design and, where supported, export or prepare a usable file.

Use **Content + design specification** when direct design production is unavailable, unsuitable, or not requested.

Never claim that a design or PDF was produced unless an actual artifact exists.

## 2. Understand the communication job
Identify from context:
- target audience;
- communication objective;
- source material and evidence requirements;
- brand identity, if supplied;
- desired CTA;
- whether the carousel is educational, analytical, promotional, explanatory, or thought leadership.

Infer minor missing details. Ask only when a missing decision materially changes the output.

## 3. Build the swipe narrative
Do not split prose mechanically across slides. Build a sequence in which every page advances the argument.

Typical arc:
1. Cover/hook
2. Tension, problem, or promise
3. Why it matters
4. Core insights
5. Evidence/example/framework
6. Practical implications
7. Synthesis
8. CTA/closing

Use only as many slides as the idea needs.

## 4. Write and design for mobile reading
Default production canvas: **4:5 portrait, 1080 × 1350 px**. Treat this as a practical design default, not a claim that LinkedIn mandates that exact PDF size.

Apply one dominant idea per slide, short headlines, controlled text density, clear hierarchy, consistent margins/grid/type, and purposeful visuals. Cross-slide elements may be used when they improve continuity.

If a brand kit, logo, colors, or typography are available, apply them consistently.

## 5. Protect evidence quality
Verify factual or time-sensitive claims when external evidence matters. Prefer primary or authoritative sources appropriate to the topic. Preserve the meaning of the source; do not invent statistics, quotations, attribution, case examples, platform rules, or citations.

## 6. Package for LinkedIn
Prefer PDF for the final document post. Supply:
- LinkedIn document title;
- accompanying post caption;
- CTA;
- source/citation notes where needed.

When current platform requirements matter, verify them against LinkedIn Help rather than relying on remembered limits.

## 7. Output contract
Deliver, as applicable:
1. Objective + audience
2. Recommended hook/title
3. Slide-by-slide copy
4. Slide-by-slide visual direction
5. Design system/specification
6. LinkedIn document title
7. LinkedIn caption
8. CTA
9. Evidence/source notes
10. Canva design or PDF when actually produced
11. QA results

## 8. Final QA
Before completion confirm:
- slide 1 earns the swipe;
- each slide advances the story;
- no page is overloaded;
- facts and quotes are traceable;
- typography is mobile-readable;
- sequence and page numbering are correct;
- brand use is consistent;
- CTA matches the objective;
- PDF pages use consistent dimensions;
- completion claims match the artifacts actually produced.

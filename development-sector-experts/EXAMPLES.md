# Usage Examples

These prompts illustrate how Development Sector Experts can support real professional workflows. Replace countries, sectors, organizations, and documents as needed.

## 1. Country and sector analysis

### Education

> Use the Education Systems Expert to analyse foundational-learning challenges in Kenya. Identify the most important system constraints, relevant institutions, recent authoritative evidence, and realistic programme entry points for an NGO.

### Public health

> Use the Public Health Expert to prepare a concise health-systems diagnostic for maternal and newborn health in Sindh, Pakistan. Distinguish access, utilization, quality, workforce, financing, and data constraints.

### Climate resilience

> Use the Climate Resilience Expert to assess climate risks affecting smallholder livelihoods in northern Ghana and identify adaptation options that avoid maladaptation.

## 2. Proposal and concept-note development

### NGO programme design

> Use the Social Protection Expert to help design a shock-responsive cash-assistance programme for flood-affected households. Map the existing national system, identify feasible delivery mechanisms, define targeting and grievance arrangements, and propose measurable outcomes.

### Consulting assignment

> Use the Water Security Expert to review the attached terms of reference for a groundwater-security assessment. Explain the implied methodology, key institutions, evidence requirements, technical risks, and likely team competencies.

### Cross-sector proposal

> Use the Development Sector Expert Router to identify the best lead and supporting experts for a climate-resilient WASH programme in Mozambique, then structure the analysis around the selected experts.

## 3. Evaluation and research

### Evaluation design

> Use the Child Protection Expert to develop an evaluation framework for a community-based child-protection programme. Include evaluation questions, child-sensitive methods, safeguarding requirements, referral preparedness, disaggregation, and key limitations.

### Conflicting statistics

> Use the Development Data Quality Expert to assess these three different estimates of out-of-school children. Compare their year, age range, geography, methodology, source, and fitness for use in a donor proposal.

### Evidence strategy

> Use the Official Evidence Source Expert to identify the strongest source hierarchy for researching youth unemployment in Jordan. Prioritize original official and institutional sources and explain what each source is suitable for.

## 4. Institutional and stakeholder analysis

> Use the Institutional Mapping Expert to map the institutions responsible for technical and vocational education in Pakistan. For each material actor, explain mandate, authority, geographic level, role in implementation, and relevance to a donor-funded skills programme.

> Use the Local Governance Expert to analyse who is responsible for solid-waste management in the target municipality and how municipal finance, provincial/state oversight, and service-provider arrangements affect implementation.

## 5. Livelihoods and economic development

> Use the Livelihoods and Economic Inclusion Expert to assess livelihood options for low-income urban women in Bangladesh. Test market demand, mobility constraints, care responsibilities, finance, skills, and resilience rather than recommending generic microenterprise activities.

> Use the Employment and Skills Expert to design a youth-employment programme linked to real employer demand. Include labour-market evidence, employer engagement, training, work-based learning, placement, retention, and job-quality indicators.

> Use the Agriculture and Rural Development Expert to review a climate-smart agriculture concept for smallholders. Check agroecological suitability, market demand, water requirements, extension capacity, gendered asset access, and net-income logic.

## 6. Governance, migration, peace and humanitarian work

> Use the Governance and Public Sector Expert to diagnose why a public-service reform is not translating into improved frontline delivery. Distinguish mandate, capacity, incentives, finance, accountability, and political-economy constraints.

> Use the Migration and Human Mobility Expert to analyse risks faced by labour migrants moving from Pakistan to Gulf countries. Cover recruitment, contracts, fees, protection, grievance, consular support, legal pathways, and data limitations.

> Use the Peacebuilding Expert to conduct a conflict-sensitivity review of this livelihoods programme. Identify dividers, connectors, targeting risks, resource-transfer effects, and changes needed to reduce unintended harm.

> Use the Humanitarian Response Expert to develop a protection-sensitive response framework for newly displaced households after flooding. Include needs, modality choice, coordination, accountability, localization, and early-recovery considerations.

## 7. Digital transformation and responsible AI

### NGO AI integration

> Use the Responsible AI for Development Expert to identify and prioritize AI use cases for a medium-sized NGO. Focus on proposal development, donor intelligence, research, M&E, knowledge management, communications, and administration. Score each use case for value, feasibility, data sensitivity, consequence of error, and human-oversight needs.

### Consulting-firm workflow

> Use the Responsible AI for Development Expert to redesign our consultancy bid-development workflow. Map the current process, identify where generative AI can create value, establish evidence and non-fabrication controls, define human approvals, and propose a pilot with measurable success criteria.

### Digital education

> Use the Digital Education Expert to assess whether a tablet-based learning programme is appropriate for rural schools. Consider pedagogy, teacher workflow, electricity, connectivity, device management, accessibility, child data, learning outcomes, and total cost of ownership.

### Digital health

> Use the Digital Health Expert to review a proposed telehealth service for rural primary care. Assess clinical workflow, referral, interoperability, provider burden, patient access, privacy, safety, connectivity, and sustainability.

## 8. Localization

> Use the Localization and Language Expert to adapt this survey developed for an international programme for use in rural Punjab, Pakistan. Preserve the research intent while correcting administrative terminology, examples, language level, units, cultural assumptions, and respondent-facing wording.

## 9. Standalone versus integrated use

### Standalone

> Use only the Education Systems Expert to review this education programme concept for Rwanda.

The Education Systems Expert should complete the task using its built-in country, evidence, data-quality, and localization safeguards.

### Integrated

> Use the Education Systems Expert as the lead. Where useful, draw on the Country Context, Institutional Mapping, Official Evidence Source, Development Data Quality, and Localization & Language experts. Produce one integrated answer rather than separate expert reports.

## 10. Good practice for users

For stronger outputs, provide:

- the country and subnational area;
- the intended audience;
- the decision or output you need;
- relevant documents or data;
- donor or institutional requirements;
- time period;
- constraints such as budget, team size, word count, or implementation horizon.

The skills are designed to make cautious assumptions when minor details are missing, but specific context improves professional usefulness.

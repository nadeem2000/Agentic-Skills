# Official Evidence Source Expert

## Purpose
Identify and prioritize the strongest available evidence for the specific claim, country, sector, geography, and time period. Prefer primary and authoritative sources over convenient secondary summaries.

## Evidence hierarchy
Apply flexibly rather than mechanically:
1. applicable government law, policy, regulation, administrative data, statistical office, ministry, regulator or central bank;
2. official intergovernmental or multilateral source with a clear mandate for the subject;
3. recognized national research or public institution;
4. peer-reviewed research directly relevant to the country and question;
5. credible institutional evaluations, surveys and technical reports;
6. high-quality secondary synthesis where primary evidence is unavailable.

Avoid blogs, unattributed statistics, SEO pages, recycled citations, and news articles when a primary source exists.

## Workflow
1. Define the exact claim or evidence need.
2. Identify the institution with primary authority for that evidence.
3. Check recency, geography, population, definitions, and methodology.
4. Prefer the latest valid source, but retain older authoritative evidence when it is the correct baseline or only comparable series.
5. Cross-check high-stakes or surprising figures when possible.
6. Return source recommendations matched to the claims they support.

## Examples of domain authority
Use contextually:
- population and census → national statistical office;
- monetary/financial system → central bank/regulator;
- education administrative data → education ministry/EMIS; international comparison → UNESCO UIS where appropriate;
- health → health ministry and WHO depending on claim;
- labour → labour-force survey/statistical office and ILO for harmonized indicators;
- migration → government migration authorities, IOM, UNHCR depending on population and question;
- climate commitments → government/UNFCCC; climate science → IPCC and relevant national institutions.

## Integrated mode
Use `development-data-quality-expert` when sources conflict or data fitness is uncertain, and `country-context-expert` when country relevance must be established.

## Standalone mode
The skill remains fully usable on its own. Apply source-quality and fit-for-purpose checks internally.

## Output options
- ranked source list with rationale;
- evidence matrix;
- source strategy for a proposal/research task;
- claim-to-source map;
- source gap note.

## Quality controls
- A prestigious source is not automatically the best source for every claim.
- Match geography, population, definition and year to the user's statement.
- Distinguish original data producers from organizations republishing the data.
- Never invent a citation, report title, URL, policy or dataset.

# Institutional Mapping Expert

## Purpose
Map the institutions that matter to a development-sector decision and explain their mandates, authority, relationships, influence, and potential role.

## Do not produce a directory
A useful institutional map answers: who matters, why they matter, what authority they hold, how they interact, and what this means for the task.

## Workflow
1. Define country, geography, sector, task, and decision purpose.
2. Identify the formal governance and service-delivery architecture.
3. Map public institutions first, then relevant non-state actors.
4. Verify mandates and avoid inferring authority from organization names.
5. Distinguish policy, regulation, financing, implementation, oversight, data, research, and convening roles.
6. Assess influence, dependencies, coordination gaps, and engagement relevance.

## Actor categories
Use as relevant:
- ministries and departments;
- statutory authorities and regulators;
- provincial/state/regional and local governments;
- public service providers and utilities;
- national statistical and research bodies;
- donors and development banks;
- UN agencies;
- NGOs and INGOs;
- civil-society networks;
- universities and think tanks;
- private-sector and professional associations;
- community and representative organizations.

## Core mapping fields
For each material actor capture:
- institution;
- mandate/function;
- level of authority;
- geographic reach;
- relevance to the task;
- relationship with other actors;
- influence/interest where useful;
- possible engagement or partnership role;
- evidence/source for mandate when available.

## Integrated mode
Use `country-context-expert`, `official-evidence-source-expert`, and `localization-and-language-expert` when they add value.

## Standalone mode
If other experts are absent, verify institutional facts using the best available authoritative evidence and continue independently.

## Output options
- institutional landscape narrative;
- stakeholder matrix;
- RACI-style responsibility map;
- power-interest matrix;
- coordination architecture;
- partnership and consultation map;
- decision pathway.

## Quality controls
- Do not assume ministries with similar names have equivalent mandates across countries.
- Distinguish legal mandate from practical influence.
- Flag institutional reforms, mergers, or uncertain responsibilities.
- Avoid long lists of actors with no analytical relevance.

# Grant & Bid Opportunity Intelligence Specialist Design

## Purpose

Create the third commercial proof point in the Agentic-Skills portfolio: a human-supervised opportunity-intelligence specialist for development organisations, NGOs/INGOs and consulting firms.

The flagship must distinguish clearly between two different opportunity types:

1. **Grant and institutional-funding opportunities** pursued through calls for proposals, calls for concept notes, grant RFPs, challenge funds, funding windows and related institutional calls.
2. **Consulting and professional-service opportunities** pursued through bids, tenders, EOIs, prequalification notices, RFPs/TORs, framework agreements and other consulting procurements.

The specialist must use a shared intelligence discipline while applying different qualification logic, evidence requirements and decision criteria to each pathway.

## Positioning

**Primary title:** Grant & Bid Opportunity Intelligence Specialist

**Supporting descriptor:** AI-supported opportunity discovery, qualification and capture for development organisations and consulting firms.

**Internal tracks:**
- **Grant Opportunity Analyst** — for NGOs, INGOs, foundations, CSOs, universities, programme organisations and resource-mobilisation teams.
- **Consulting Bid Analyst** — for development consulting firms, evaluation firms, research/advisory companies and technical-service providers.

Avoid machine-centric labels such as **engine**. Prefer professional role language such as **specialist**, **analyst**, **expert**, **adviser** and **reviewer** where appropriate.

## Portfolio Role

The flagship sits upstream of the existing AI Proposal & Bid Development Workflow.

```text
Opportunity Intelligence
        ↓
Human pursue / do-not-pursue decision
        ↓
Proposal / Concept Note / Bid Development
        ↓
Submission
```

It must not duplicate the downstream proposal-development workflow. Its core purpose is to help organisations find, verify, understand, qualify, prioritise and prepare for the right opportunities before committing proposal-development resources.

## Shared Intelligence Discipline

Both tracks follow the same high-level logic:

**Discover → Verify → Classify → Qualify → Match → Prioritise → Capture → Human decision**

However, the underlying questions differ by track.

### Grant qualification question

> Are we eligible, strategically aligned, credible to deliver, appropriately partnered and competitive for this funding opportunity?

### Consulting-bid qualification question

> Can we satisfy the procurement requirements, demonstrate relevant corporate experience, mobilise the required experts and partners, manage delivery risk, and compete credibly for the assignment?

The final implementation must make this distinction visually and textually explicit.

# Track A — Grant Opportunity Analyst

## Primary users

- NGOs and INGOs;
- foundations and civil-society organisations;
- universities and research institutions;
- resource-mobilisation and institutional-funding teams;
- programme-development teams;
- partnership and grants teams.

## Typical opportunity formats

- Call for Proposals;
- Call for Concept Notes;
- grant RFP;
- challenge fund;
- innovation fund;
- institutional funding window;
- partnership funding call;
- thematic or country programme call;
- restricted or invited grant opportunity where relevant.

Terminology must reflect the source document rather than forcing every grant opportunity into a generic "RFP" label.

## Grant workflow

### Stage A1 — Opportunity discovery

AI-supported tasks:
- structure searches across approved donor and institutional sources;
- classify opportunities by donor, theme, geography, instrument and deadline;
- remove obvious duplicates;
- create a preliminary opportunity register.

Human responsibility:
- define target donor/sector/geography parameters;
- approve the opportunity sources being monitored;
- decide which opportunities merit verification.

### Stage A2 — Official-source verification

AI-supported tasks:
- extract the official source URL or document reference;
- capture deadline, funding range, duration and key dates;
- identify authoritative guidance and annexes;
- flag conflicts between secondary alerts and official documents.

Human responsibility:
- confirm material information against official donor/institutional sources;
- resolve discrepancies before internal circulation.

### Stage A3 — Eligibility assessment

AI-supported tasks:
- extract applicant eligibility;
- identify country/geographic restrictions;
- identify registration, legal-status or consortium conditions;
- identify co-financing or cost-share requirements where stated;
- identify mandatory prior experience or thematic requirements;
- create an eligibility matrix.

Human responsibility:
- make the authoritative eligibility determination;
- seek donor clarification where rules are ambiguous.

### Stage A4 — Strategic fit

AI-supported tasks:
- compare donor priorities with organisational strategy;
- assess thematic and geographic alignment;
- compare intended beneficiary groups and delivery model;
- identify where an opportunity would stretch beyond current mandate.

Human responsibility:
- determine strategic desirability;
- prevent opportunity chasing that is inconsistent with mission or capacity.

### Stage A5 — Organisational capability and evidence match

AI-supported tasks:
- map verified organisational experience to the call;
- identify relevant projects, results, partnerships and technical strengths from approved records;
- identify evidence gaps;
- distinguish documented capability from aspirational capability.

Human responsibility:
- approve all organisational claims;
- determine whether capability gaps are acceptable or require partners.

### Stage A6 — Partnership and consortium requirements

AI-supported tasks:
- identify mandatory or advantageous partnership conditions;
- map partner competency gaps;
- structure partner-search criteria;
- prepare a preliminary consortium rationale.

Human responsibility:
- select partners;
- evaluate reputational, delivery and fiduciary risk;
- approve consortium roles and relationships.

### Stage A7 — Funding and submission pathway

AI-supported tasks:
- summarise funding ceiling/range, duration and cost-share requirements;
- identify concept-note vs full-proposal stages;
- identify annexes, templates and submission portal requirements;
- map internal actions and deadlines.

Human responsibility:
- validate financial implications;
- approve whether the organisation can meet the submission and co-financing conditions.

### Stage A8 — Grant opportunity prioritisation

AI-supported tasks:
- create a transparent fit/feasibility scoring structure;
- compare eligible opportunities using approved criteria;
- surface assumptions and uncertainties;
- prepare a Grant Opportunity Brief.

Human responsibility:
- make the pursue / do-not-pursue / monitor decision;
- decide resource allocation and capture ownership.

## Grant Opportunity Brief — minimum output

The flagship should demonstrate a reusable brief containing:
- opportunity title and donor;
- official source;
- opportunity type;
- deadline and key dates;
- geography;
- thematic scope;
- funding amount/range;
- duration;
- eligibility;
- concept-note/full-proposal pathway;
- strategic fit;
- relevant organisational capability;
- evidence gaps;
- partnership requirements;
- key risks;
- recommended next action;
- human decision/status.

# Track B — Consulting Bid Analyst

## Primary users

- international-development consulting firms;
- evaluation firms;
- research and advisory companies;
- technical assistance providers;
- engineering/social-development consultancies where relevant;
- bid, capture and business-development teams.

## Typical opportunity formats

- bid or tender notice;
- consulting RFP;
- TOR-based assignment;
- EOI;
- prequalification notice;
- framework agreement;
- roster/framework opportunity;
- donor or government consulting procurement.

## Consulting-bid workflow

### Stage B1 — Opportunity discovery

AI-supported tasks:
- structure monitoring across approved procurement sources;
- classify assignments by buyer, sector, geography, service type and deadline;
- create an opportunity register;
- distinguish consulting assignments from grants and goods/works procurements.

Human responsibility:
- define target markets and buyers;
- approve source coverage and strategic search parameters.

### Stage B2 — Procurement-source verification

AI-supported tasks:
- identify the authoritative notice, TOR/RFP and amendments;
- capture procurement method and submission stage;
- extract deadlines and clarification dates;
- flag outdated or secondary-source notices.

Human responsibility:
- confirm current procurement requirements from the original source;
- validate amendments and submission instructions.

### Stage B3 — Eligibility and mandatory requirements

AI-supported tasks:
- extract firm eligibility;
- identify legal/registration requirements;
- identify geographic or nationality restrictions;
- identify mandatory certifications, turnover or experience thresholds where stated;
- identify consortium/JV rules;
- create a compliance-oriented eligibility summary.

Human responsibility:
- determine authoritative compliance;
- involve legal/procurement expertise where needed.

### Stage B4 — Scope and TOR decomposition

AI-supported tasks:
- break the assignment into work packages;
- identify deliverables, locations, timing and implied level of effort;
- identify evaluation criteria where stated;
- distinguish mandatory scope from possible interpretation;
- identify clarification questions.

Human responsibility:
- approve the interpretation of scope and delivery implications;
- decide whether ambiguities create unacceptable bid risk.

### Stage B5 — Corporate experience match

AI-supported tasks:
- map verified assignments to sector, service type, geography, client and methodology requirements;
- identify strong references and capability gaps;
- create a source-linked corporate-experience matrix.

Human responsibility:
- approve project claims and reference use;
- decide whether experience is sufficiently relevant and competitive.

### Stage B6 — Expert and staffing implications

AI-supported tasks:
- extract required roles and competency requirements;
- map available verified experts against role requirements;
- identify recruitment, partner or associate gaps;
- flag unrealistic mobilisation assumptions.

Human responsibility:
- confirm expert availability and consent;
- approve staffing and mobilisation strategy.

### Stage B7 — Partner, consortium and delivery-risk assessment

AI-supported tasks:
- identify local-presence or consortium needs;
- map missing competencies;
- structure delivery, security, operational and schedule risks;
- identify dependencies requiring capture action.

Human responsibility:
- select partners and experts;
- assess reputational and commercial risk;
- decide whether delivery conditions are acceptable.

### Stage B8 — Bid prioritisation and capture

AI-supported tasks:
- prepare a transparent opportunity score based on approved criteria;
- surface reasons to bid and reasons not to bid;
- produce a Bid Intelligence Brief;
- structure next actions for capture, partner outreach, expert mobilisation and proposal preparation.

Human responsibility:
- make the bid/no-bid decision;
- approve commercial resource commitment and capture strategy.

## Bid Intelligence Brief — minimum output

The flagship should demonstrate a reusable brief containing:
- opportunity title and buyer/client;
- official procurement source;
- procurement type/stage;
- deadline and clarification dates;
- geography and assignment location;
- sector/service type;
- scope and deliverables;
- eligibility and mandatory conditions;
- evaluation criteria where stated;
- relevant corporate experience;
- expert requirements;
- consortium/partner gaps;
- delivery and commercial risks;
- recommended next action;
- human bid/no-bid decision/status.

# Shared Human-Control Framework

The implementation must clearly separate AI-supported analysis from human decisions.

AI must not autonomously:
- declare legal eligibility;
- commit an organisation to pursue an opportunity;
- contact or appoint partners;
- confirm expert availability;
- make organisational experience claims without approved evidence;
- determine budgets or commercial terms;
- submit an application, concept note, proposal, EOI or bid;
- treat probability scores as objective likelihood of winning.

## Risk and Control Matrix — required topics

The final flagship must address at minimum:
- stale opportunity information;
- incorrect deadlines;
- secondary-source errors;
- eligibility misinterpretation;
- fabricated organisational experience;
- invented expert availability;
- inappropriate grant/bid classification;
- hidden consortium or co-financing conditions;
- overconfident scoring;
- biased opportunity prioritisation;
- confidential pipeline information;
- opportunity chasing outside organisational mandate;
- duplication across opportunity sources.

## Scoring principles

Any opportunity score must be transparent and decomposable. It must not be presented as a model-generated "probability of winning" unless supported by a separately validated forecasting method.

Suggested dimensions may include:

### Grant track
- eligibility;
- strategic alignment;
- thematic/geographic fit;
- demonstrated capability;
- partnership readiness;
- funding/implementation feasibility;
- submission feasibility;
- competition/uncertainty as a qualitative consideration where evidence exists.

### Consulting-bid track
- eligibility/compliance;
- corporate experience fit;
- technical scope fit;
- expert availability;
- local/partner readiness;
- delivery feasibility;
- commercial attractiveness where management supplies relevant inputs;
- strategic client/market value.

Scores are decision-support artefacts. Management retains the final decision.

## Claims Policy

Do not claim that the specialist:
- guarantees opportunity discovery completeness;
- guarantees eligibility;
- predicts wins reliably;
- improves win rates by a stated percentage;
- saves a stated amount of time without measured pilot evidence;
- replaces business-development, grants, capture or procurement professionals.

Use evidence-safe language such as **can support**, **potential benefit**, **human-supervised**, **subject to official-source verification**, and **should be measured during a pilot**.

## Suggested Pilot

Pilot separately on one grant pipeline and one consulting-bid pipeline.

### Grant pilot
- review a defined set of official donor/institutional sources;
- identify and classify opportunities;
- produce 5–10 Grant Opportunity Briefs;
- compare analyst recommendations with human grant-team decisions.

### Consulting-bid pilot
- review a defined set of procurement sources;
- identify and classify consulting opportunities;
- produce 5–10 Bid Intelligence Briefs;
- compare analyst recommendations with human BD/capture decisions.

### Suggested metrics
- percentage of surfaced opportunities correctly classified;
- number of material source-verification corrections;
- number of eligibility interpretation corrections;
- number of duplicate opportunities detected;
- proportion of briefs requiring major human rewrite;
- number of capability/partner/expert gaps identified before pursuit;
- user-rated usefulness;
- analyst decision agreement/disagreement with human review;
- sensitive-data or confidentiality incidents;
- reusable intelligence records created.

## Success Criteria

The flagship is successful when:

1. A reader immediately understands that grants and consulting bids follow different pathways.
2. The flagship uses professional human-role terminology rather than machine-centric labels.
3. Grant users can see a credible workflow for calls for proposals/concept notes and related institutional funding.
4. Consulting firms can see a credible workflow for bids, tenders, EOIs and TOR/RFP-based assignments.
5. Opportunity qualification remains clearly upstream of proposal/bid development.
6. Every consequential recommendation has a human decision point.
7. All material opportunity facts are subject to official-source verification.
8. No win-rate, ROI or efficiency claims are presented without measured evidence.
9. The flagship is commercially understandable to NGO resource-mobilisation leaders and consulting-firm BD/capture leaders.

## Out of Scope for This Version

This version will not build:
- an autonomous web crawler;
- a software dashboard;
- automated portal submission;
- autonomous partner outreach;
- automated proposal writing as part of this flagship;
- machine-generated win-probability forecasting;
- commercial pricing models;
- CRM or procurement-platform integrations.

Those capabilities may be considered later only where they add clear organisational value and can be implemented responsibly.
